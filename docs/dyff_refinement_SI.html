Supporting Information: Refining a rule-based force field (DYFF) with GFN2-xTB calculations and DFT reference libraries in EasyHybrid

Summary. On the test half of the SPICE capped dipeptides (B3LYP-D3BJ/DZVP), the median heavy-atom force RMSE is 75.5 kcal/mol/Å for DYFF with its rules, 33.3 with the GFN2-xTB residue library, 31.7 with the DFT-calibrated library and 21.8 with the force-matched library, against 23.1 for OPLS-AA and 18.6 for GFN2-xTB itself (Tables S8, S9). The force-matched model is better than OPLS-AA for forces and for the relative energies of distorted geometries, and worse for the energy differences between relaxed conformers (4.07 vs 2.30 kcal/mol), a gap that is not in the angle and torsion terms (Table S10). Independently, the DFT calibration of the Hessian constants halves the error of harmonic frequencies (Table S3), and per-bond torsion corrections looked up in a DFT library by chemical environment are as accurate as, or more accurate than, a GFN2-xTB scan of the molecule itself (Table S5).

S1. Overview and software

DYFF, the rule-based force field of pDynamo3, assigns every bonded parameter from atom types, bond orders and periodic-table data, so it covers the whole periodic table, including transition metals. This work adds layers of corrections that keep that coverage while bringing energies and forces closer to quantum-chemical references. Each layer was validated on data not used to build it.

  1. bond and angle constants from the GFN2-xTB Hessian of the molecule (modified Seminario), calibrated to DFT by chemical environment;
  2. per-bond torsion corrections, either looked up in a library of DFT torsion scans by chemical environment, or fitted to a GFN2-xTB scan of the molecule;
  3. CM5 partial charges with a dedicated small Lennard-Jones type for polar hydrogens;
  4. a library of 71 amino-acid templates (charges, bonded terms, backbone and side-chain torsions), optionally force-matched to DFT dipeptide data.

Software: EasyHybrid 3 (commit 7ede5eb + this work), pDynamo3 (commit da6e90f), xtb 6.7.1, RDKit 2025.09.1 (EasyHybrid environment; environment codes are computed there), NumPy 1.21.5, networkx 3.4.2; data import in a separate environment with qcportal 0.70, RDKit 2026.03.6, NumPy 2.2.6. Python 3.10.12.

S2. Reference data

All quantum-chemical reference data are public records of the QCArchive server (https://api.qcarchive.molssi.org) [1], most of them submitted by the Open Force Field Initiative [2]; they were fetched with qcportal without authentication. Level of theory: B3LYP-D3(BJ)/DZVP [3,4] throughout. Atom order is defined by the map numbers of the canonical mapped SMILES stored with every record (atom i has map number i+1); geometries are converted from bohr to Å. A scan whose bonding changes along the profile (for instance a proton transfer, detected from the distances) is excluded.

SPICE dipeptides: 8124 geometries (first, middle and last step of 2708 geometry optimizations) of 677 capped dipeptides, from the SPICE Dipeptides Partial Relaxation Dataset v4.0 (QCArchive id 414, B3LYP-D3BJ/DZVP).

The SPICE dipeptides [5] are capped ACE-X-Y-NME with X, Y among 26 residue codes (protonation variants included). For each of the 677 dipeptides, 4 of its 50 optimization records were chosen with a fixed random seed, and the first, middle and last steps of each trajectory were taken: distorted and relaxed geometries, each with its DFT energy and gradient. The dipeptides are divided into a training half and a test half by a fixed hash of the name. The force matching (S3.9) uses only the training half; every number in Table S8 is computed on the test half.

S3. Methods

S3.1 DYFF with edited parameters

DYFF creates each parameter with a factory that receives the atom indices of the term and returns a value and a key; terms with the same key share the parameter. A subclass of the pDynamo DYFF model wraps the factories and replaces values by key (all terms of a type) or per term: a bond a-b, an angle a-b-c, all dihedral terms around a bond j-k, and the three out-of-plane terms of a centre j. A per-term override returns a key extended with the atom labels, so the parameter is not shared with other terms of the same type. Per-term edits store a topology signature (atom count and a hash of the bond list) and are dropped if the molecule changes. Functional forms (kJ/mol): bond E = k(b − b0)²; angle and dihedral E = Σn cn cos(nθ); DYFF 1-4 scaling 0.5 for both electrostatics and Lennard-Jones.

S3.2 Bonds and angles from the Hessian (modified Seminario)

The Hessian comes from xtb --ohess (GFN2-xTB [6] at the GFN2 minimum; ALPB water [7] for ions). Bond constants follow Seminario [8]: the 3×3 block −Hab is projected on the bond direction. Angle constants follow the modified method of Allen et al. [9], which corrects the original's overestimate at atoms with several neighbours. The equilibrium values are those of the GFN2 minimum. The angle constant K at θ0 is converted into the DYFF cosine series with the general UFF form, whose curvature at θ0 equals K; near-linear angles use K(1 + cos θ). Terms with non-physical constants (k < 100 kJ mol⁻¹ Å⁻² or K < 10 kJ mol⁻¹ rad⁻²) keep the DYFF value; this happens, for example, for the C–COO⁻ bond of gas-phase carboxylates.

S3.3 Chemical environment codes

Each term (bond: 2 atoms; angle: 3) gets three codes:

  • exact: canonical isomeric SMILES of the fragment plus the canonical symmetry classes of the term atoms, direction-normalized;
  • r2 and r1: ECFP-like identifiers of the term atoms after 2 or 1 rounds of neighbour hashing, plus the bond orders along the term. The atom invariants are explicit (element, aromaticity, degree, H count, formal charge, ring membership, hybridization), so they do not depend on RDKit defaults.

The EasyHybrid implementation builds the RDKit molecule from atomic numbers, Kekulé bond orders and formal charges, with stereochemistry assigned from the 3D coordinates. It reproduces the library codes: 800/800 database molecules for r1/r2, plus unit tests on bonds and angles. Library lookups go exact → r2 → r1.

S3.4 Calibration of bond and angle constants by environment

For the 928 molecules with DFT Hessians, Seminario constants were computed from both the DFT Hessian (at the DFT minimum) and the GFN2-xTB Hessian (at the GFN2 minimum). For every environment code c, the table stores the means

ρc = ⟨kDFT/kxTB⟩,   Δc = ⟨v0,DFT − v0,xTB⟩

The constants of a new molecule, from its own GFN2-xTB Hessian, become k·ρc and v0 + Δc. This corrects the method bias and keeps the molecule's own geometry. The validation used a 2:1 split by molecule (Table S3); the table shipped with EasyHybrid uses all 928 molecules.

S3.5 Per-bond torsion fit (self-consistent)

For a rotatable bond j-k, one series Σn=1..4 cn cos(nφ) replaces every DYFF dihedral term about the bond and is applied to all quadruples i-j-k-l. The fit proceeds as follows:

  1. run a relaxed reference scan every 15° (24 points);
  2. run a DYFF scan restrained to the same angles (harmonic restraint 1000 kJ/mol/rad², LBFGS to an RMS gradient of 0.01 kJ/mol/Å), with the current correction, which is then subtracted;
  3. solve a weighted linear least-squares problem for the residual profile on the relaxed geometries, with higher-energy points down-weighted and mutually cancelling periods dropped;
  4. repeat steps 2–3 for three rounds, as in ffTK [10] and ForceBalance [11].

A first version used unconverged minimizations (500 conjugate-gradient steps) and a single round; it overstated the DYFF error and understated the transfer. All numbers reported here use the converged, iterated protocol.

S3.6 DFT torsion library

Every B3LYP-D3BJ/DZVP 1D scan (17 939 after exclusions) was fitted as in S3.5. The base model was DYFF plus (uncalibrated) Seminario bonds and angles from the GFN2-xTB Hessian of the fragment. Two tables were built: without partial charges, and with GFN1-xTB CM5 charges [12] plus the polar-H LJ type. A table entry is the period-by-period mean of all fits sharing a code. For the transfer test, every scan of a test molecule was excluded from training (Tables S5, S6).

S3.7 Capped subsystems

To refine a selection or the atoms outside the residue library, a region is grown by s bonds (s = 3 for torsions; 2 for charges and Hessians), then extended so that no multiple, aromatic or small-ring bond (≤ 8-membered) is cut, a metal keeps its ligands, and a metal bonded to the region is included. Open valences are capped with hydrogens. Larger cycles are cut: in proteins, disulfide bridges close macrocycles through the backbone, and an earlier version that refused to cut them grew a 2-shell region around a bridged cysteine to 331 atoms instead of 90. Only terms whose atoms are all in the selection are refined. Atoms outside the residue library are treated per connected group.

S3.8 Residue library

The 71 templates are:

  • 26 internal residues (ACE-X-NME), including protonation variants;
  • 22 N-terminal (NH₃⁺-X-NME) and 22 C-terminal (ACE-X-COO⁻) residues;
  • a disulfide-bonded cysteine, CYX (ACE-CYS-NME with S–S–CH₃).

Each template has two backbone conformers (α: φ −60°, ψ −45°; β: −120°, 130°), started at the target angles with MMFF and optimized with the backbone dihedrals restrained (GFN2-xTB, ALPB water). Template parameters:

  • CM5 charges (GFN1-xTB, ALPB water), averaged over the conformers, symmetrized over equivalent atoms and shifted to the integer residue charge;
  • Seminario bonds and angles averaged over the conformers, optionally calibrated (S3.4);
  • φ, ψ, ω and side-chain χ fitted as in S3.5 on the β conformer.

Matching uses graph isomorphism within a residue family. A residue is rejected if a non-backbone atom is bonded outside it, except the S of CYX, whose partner S fills a dedicated junction. This rule also keeps a disulfide-bonded cysteine from being taken for a thiolate.

S3.9 Force matching of the residue templates

Fitted parameters, shared by every occurrence of a template:

  • for each internal angle term, a scale s, so that E = s·Etemplate(θ);
  • for each dihedral bond, the coefficients c1..4.

The model energy is linear in all of them. With Ep the basis function of parameter p, and the rest of the model fixed (charges, LJ, bonds, junction terms, caps), the fit minimizes

Σframes |FDFT − Ffixed + Σp θp∇Ep|² / σF² + w Σframes (ΔEDFT − ΔEfixed − Σp θpΔEp)² / σE² + λ |θ − θ0|²

Δ denotes a value centered within its dipeptide, and θ0 the base template values. Settings: σF = 50 kJ/mol/Å, σE = 1 kcal/mol, w = 30. The ridge strength λ is chosen on a validation quarter of the training half. Analytic gradients of the angle and dihedral basis functions were checked against finite differences (agreement to 10⁻⁹). Angle scales are kept ≥ 0.2.

S3.10 Polar-hydrogen Lennard-Jones type

DYFF gives every hydrogen σ = 2.571 Å, ε = 0.184 kJ/mol. With partial charges, a hydrogen bond puts a polar H about 1.9 Å from its acceptor, inside that repulsive wall. Hydrogens bonded to N, O or S therefore get their own type (σ = 1.0 Å, ε = 0.192 kJ/mol, similar to CHARMM), in both the full and the 1-4 tables. The type is applied only when the model has partial charges, because without electrostatics nothing would keep the hydrogen off the acceptor.

S4. Metrics

  • Torsion profile RMSE: the relaxed DYFF scan (S3.5) against the reference profile, both shifted by a weighted mean offset (kcal/mol). "Same minimum" means the global minima are within one scan step.
  • Frequencies: the model is minimized from the DFT geometry and its finite-difference Hessian is diagonalized with atomic masses. RMSE is taken over the sorted 3N−6 frequencies against the DFT ones, also restricted to DFT modes below 1000 cm⁻¹. Bond and angle MAE are computed at the model minimum.
  • Force RMSE of a geometry: √(⟨|Fmodel − Fref|²⟩atoms), over all atoms and over heavy atoms. Forces are evaluated at the reference geometry; a dipeptide's value is the mean over its geometries.
  • Relative energy RMSE of a dipeptide: the energies of its geometries, each centered on its own mean, compared over all geometries and over the relaxed (last-step) geometries only. The latter is the error in conformational energy differences.
  • Statistics: medians over the independent units (scans, molecules, dipeptides), with 95 % percentile bootstrap intervals (2000 resamples of those units).

S6. Limitations

  • All DFT references are gas-phase B3LYP-D3BJ/DZVP, the OpenFF level, chosen for its data volume. Its own errors relative to higher levels are not addressed here.
  • Partial charges make gas-phase torsion profiles worse (Table S6). The charged model is meant for condensed-phase work, which is not benchmarked here.
  • QCArchive provides no transition-metal reference. Metal-containing systems use the same machinery (Hessian, subsystems), but are not validated against DFT here.
  • The non-bonded parameters (beyond the polar-H type) were not refit; no intermolecular interaction energies were used.
  • The torsion library was fitted on top of uncalibrated Seminario bonds and angles (S3.6). EasyHybrid now applies the calibration (S3.4) before the torsion corrections, so the base differs slightly from the one used in training. Refitting the library on the calibrated base is planned.
  • Environment codes have limited coverage: 89 % of the rotatable bonds of a 406-molecule structure library, and 84 % of bonds and 70 % of angles of held-out Hessian molecules. Uncovered terms fall back to the uncorrected GFN2-xTB or DYFF values.
